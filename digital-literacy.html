Digital Literacy Basics

Master essential computer hardware, desktop navigation, web browsing, and personal online safety.

Lesson 1: Introduction to Computers

A computer is an electronic device that processes data, converting raw inputs into useful information. It consists of hardware (physical parts like the CPU, monitor, keyboard, and storage) and software (programs and operating systems like Windows or Linux).

  • Central Processing Unit (CPU): The brain of the computer that executes instructions.
  • RAM (Memory): Temporary workspace used for running programs quickly.
  • Storage Drive (SSD/HDD): Long-term storage where your files and operating system live.

Lesson 2: Keyboard & Mouse Navigation

Interacting with your computer efficiently requires mastering pointing and typing devices:

  • Left-Click: Selects items or opens buttons.
  • Right-Click: Opens contextual menus with extra options.
  • Tab Key: Navigates sequentially through clickable items on web pages and windows.
  • Enter Key: Triggers selected links, submits forms, or creates new lines of text.

Lesson 3: Understanding the Internet

The Internet is a global network connecting millions of devices worldwide. Web pages are accessed using a web browser (like Chrome, Firefox, or Edge) by typing a URL (Uniform Resource Locator) into the address bar.

Lesson 4: Internet Safety

Protecting your personal information online is critical:

  • Use strong, unique passwords containing letters, numbers, and symbols.
  • Never share personal financial information or passwords over unencrypted channels.
  • Be cautious of suspicious emails asking you to click unknown links (phishing).
